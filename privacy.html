1. Who we are

The Well Testing Suite is made by H2Oil Engineering, a company licensed in Dubai, United Arab Emirates. In this notice, "H2Oil", "we", "us" and "our" mean that company.

For the personal data described in this notice we are the controller: we decide why and how it is used. The exception is data you store for an employer or client, where we act for them (section 15).

We have not appointed a Data Protection Officer. For anything about privacy, write to software@h2oil.co.uk.

2. What this notice covers

  • The web app (the Well Testing Suite at welltest.h2oil.co.uk), including its help pages.
  • The iOS app "Well Testing Suite", which is free.
  • Web accounts and cloud projects, an optional part of the web app. Accounts are in an invitation-only trial and will be offered to more users in stages. Until accounts are offered to you, the account sections (7, 8 and the account rows of the tables) do not apply to you.
  • The WTS Modbus Bridge, a small program you can install to connect the web app to equipment on your own network.
  • E-mails you send us.

Without an account, you do not need to give us any personal data to use the Well Testing Suite. Our hosting provider still sees technical details of each visit to the website, such as your IP address (section 6).

3. Laws that apply to you

People use the Well Testing Suite all over the world. We follow the data protection law that applies to us and, where their law reaches us, the law that protects you:

  • UAE law (everyone). As a company licensed in Dubai, we are subject to the data-protection law of the United Arab Emirates that applies to us. It applies to our processing of everyone's personal data, wherever they live.
  • UK and EU law (people in the UK or the EU). Because we offer the service to people in the UK and the EU, the UK GDPR, the UK Data Protection Act 2018 and the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) also apply to our processing of their personal data. So do the UK and EU rules on cookies and similar technologies.
  • Other countries. If you live elsewhere, your local law may give you further rights. We will respect them. Contact us if you want to use them.

This notice is written to meet all of these laws. Where they differ, section 10 shows the legal basis we rely on under each, and section 17 lists the rights you have under any of them.

4. Data that stays on your device

Every calculator, analysis, simulation and report is computed in your browser or in the app on your device. There is no calculation on our servers.

What you enter is saved on your device so it is still there next time:

  • calculator inputs, analyses, loaded gauge data, wells and results;
  • the Client Info page, which can hold names and e-mail addresses of client contacts and of the engineer, a client logo and well details;
  • build-up surveillance results, including the names entered at sign-off;
  • live-data (Modbus) settings and the historian database of tag values and alarms (by default the historian keeps 30 days);
  • report snapshots, report templates and your preferences, such as units and layout.

We cannot see, access or recover this data. It stays until you clear it, for example by clearing the browser's site data for welltest.h2oil.co.uk or by deleting the iOS app. Project files and PDF, PNG, CSV, Excel and database exports are made only when you ask, and go only where you save or share them.

Because we do not receive this data, we are not its controller. If you enter other people's details, such as client contacts, you are responsible for doing so lawfully.

If you use a shared or public computer, anyone using the same browser profile can see what is stored there. Clear the site data when you finish.

5. Analytics (web version only, with your consent)

The web version can use Google Analytics, a service provided by Google, to count how the app is used, so that we can see which features matter and improve them. We use it only if you accept.

Your choice

  • When you first open the web app, a banner asks whether you accept analytics cookies. "Accept" and "Reject" are equally easy. Until you accept, the Google Analytics code is not loaded, no analytics cookie is set and nothing is sent. Nothing you did before accepting is sent afterwards: the app keeps no analytics events from before your choice.
  • We remember your choice on your device for 12 months, and ask again after that or when this notice changes.
  • To change your choice, open Privacy (in the Overview group of the menu, or the "Privacy — how your data is stored" link at the bottom of the sidebar) and choose Cookie settings. If you withdraw consent, the app stops sending analytics straight away and deletes the analytics cookies it can reach. Clearing the site data for welltest.h2oil.co.uk also resets the choice.
  • Rejecting analytics does not affect any feature. Blocking it with a browser setting or content blocker has the same effect.

What is sent if you accept

  • Events such as "page viewed", "time spent on a module", "calculate clicked", "exported as PDF", "PRiSM tab opened", scroll depth and clicks on links that leave the app.
  • Each event carries only page or route names, fixed button, tool or model names, the visible label of the menu item or link clicked, and a few counters (tab number, scroll percentage, seconds on a page). For an outgoing link, the address is sent without its query string or fragment.
  • A filter in the page removes everything else before an event is sent. Never sent: input values, results or other numbers from your data, client or well names, file contents, and any text you type, search for or copy.
  • The in-app page "GA4 Events Reference" lists every event.

What Google Analytics collects itself

Once loaded, the Google Analytics code also collects some data on its own: a random identifier stored in a cookie, the page address, the referring page, the browser and device type, screen size, language, and an approximate location (country or city) worked out from your IP address. Google states that Google Analytics does not log or store IP addresses. It also records some events of its own, such as the start of a visit, a first visit and how long the page is in use. These standard fields do not pass through our filter.

Google Analytics keeps this data for up to 14 months. Google provides Google Analytics to us under its own terms for the service, and explains how it handles data from sites that use its services in its own privacy information.

When analytics are never used

  • In the iOS app. The analytics code and the banner are removed when the app is built.
  • While you are signed in to a web account. The analytics code is not loaded on a signed-in page, and no account information is ever sent to Google Analytics.

6. Visiting the website and other network requests

  • Visiting the website. Whenever your browser loads the web app, its help pages or these pages, or talks to the account server, our hosting provider processes your full IP address, the address requested, the time and your browser details (user agent). It needs them to deliver the site and to keep it secure, for example to detect attacks. These standard request logs are kept by the hosting provider for the period its own log retention sets. This happens with or without an account. Only our account database shortens IP addresses (section 7); the hosting provider's logs do not.
  • Security reports. Your browser may automatically send us a short report when the site's security policy blocks something on the page. We keep only counts by type of block and blocked host, and a rate-limit counter keyed by your shortened IP address, for up to 7 days. We never keep the page address, a sample of the content or your full IP address from these reports.
  • Code libraries. Some features use open-source libraries: the 3D view, spreadsheet import and export, and the historian database. In the web version, your browser downloads these from public content-delivery networks. It may download them in the background when you first open the app, so the features also work offline, or when you first use a feature. These are plain file downloads that carry none of your data, but the network operator sees your IP address and browser type, as for any download. Each library is checked against a fixed fingerprint before it is used. The iOS app uses the copies built into the app.
  • Offline copy. The web version keeps a copy of the app, its help pages and these libraries in your browser so it works offline. This copy contains code and pictures only.
  • Live data (Modbus). Live data goes only to the equipment you configure. In the web version it passes through the WTS Modbus Bridge, which you run on your own computer or network. The bridge connects only to the devices the app asks for (by default any device on your network, or only the addresses you list) and to the app in your browser. It sends nothing to us or to anyone else. It keeps a small log file on your own computer (start-up settings and error messages; connection details only in verbose mode), which is never sent anywhere. In the iOS app the connection is direct on your local network, after iOS asks for Local Network permission. Writing to equipment is off by default.
  • Links to installers or other pages are ordinary links that you choose to follow.

7. Web accounts and cloud projects (optional, web version only)

You can use the whole Well Testing Suite without an account. An account lets you save projects to the cloud and open them on another computer. Accounts are free. Sign-in has no password: you sign in with a code we e-mail to you, or with Google or LinkedIn (section 8).

What we store if you create an account

  • Your e-mail address, and a recovery address if you add one. We store each address encrypted, together with a one-way keyed fingerprint so we can find your account. We decrypt an address only to send you e-mail.
  • Account records: when the account was created and when you last signed in, how you signed up, which versions of the Terms of Use and this notice you accepted and when, your storage use, and a pending e-mail change. After a change, your previous address is kept, encrypted, so you can undo the change within 7 days. It is removed at the latest when you change your address again or delete your account.
  • Sign-in sessions and devices: when each session started and was last used, a device identifier we issue, an optional device name (encrypted), the type of browser (desktop or mobile, not the full browser details) and a shortened IP address. For IPv4 we keep the first three parts (/24); for IPv6, the first 48 bits.
  • Linked Google or LinkedIn sign-in, if you use one: see section 8.
  • Your cloud projects, only the ones you choose to save. They can contain page inputs, analyses, wells, build-up surveillance data, report snapshots and templates, and the Client Info page (which may include client contacts' names and e-mail addresses and a logo). Project contents are compressed and encrypted with a key for your account (AES-256-GCM) before they are stored. Project names and details are encrypted too. We also keep a history of earlier saves (section 14).
  • Your library: the curves, presets and column mappings you saved in PRiSM, kept once for your account (encrypted in the same way) and shared by your projects and devices.
  • A security log of sign-ins, sign-in method changes, exports, deletions and similar events, with the device, the browser type and the shortened IP address. It never contains your project contents or your e-mail address.
  • Short-lived records: sign-in codes (only as a keyed fingerprint, never the code itself), sign-in attempts with Google or LinkedIn, e-mails waiting to be sent, and counters that limit how often things can be tried.

If you start signing in but do not create an account

If you ask for a sign-in code, or start a Google or LinkedIn sign-in, but do not create an account, we keep your e-mail address (encrypted, only to send the code, plus a keyed fingerprint of it) and a shortened IP address for up to 24 hours, and the rate-limit counters (keyed fingerprints of the address and the shortened IP address) for 7 days. Then they are deleted.

What we do not do

  • We do not look at your project contents. They are used only to store them and give them back to you. Support staff can view account records only through audited tools, and each lookup is written to your security log.
  • We never use project data for analytics, advertising or AI training, and we never share or sell it.
  • We send no marketing e-mail.

E-mails we send

Account e-mails come from no-reply@h2oil.co.uk. They are sign-in codes and security notices, for example a sign-in on a new device, a changed e-mail address, a linked or unlinked sign-in method, an export, a deletion request, unusual activity, or a warning before an inactive account is deleted. They contain only fixed wording, times, a general device description and masked addresses. These e-mails are part of the account service, and you cannot turn them off while you have an account.

Export and deletion

  • Export everything from the Account page at any time. You get a zip file with one project file per cloud project and an account file (profile, consents, devices, linked sign-in methods and your own security log). The export does not yet include your library. A copy of it stays in each browser where you use your account, and we will send you a copy on request.
  • Delete your account from the Account page. We ask for a fresh e-mail code first. Your account and data are hidden at once, all sessions end and any Google or LinkedIn link is removed immediately. You can cancel within 7 days by signing in with an e-mail code; Google or LinkedIn links do not come back, so link them again if you want them. When the 7 days end, or within a day if you choose "delete immediately", your data is removed from our live systems, and it disappears from backups within 30 days after that. If we ever restore a backup, deleted accounts are deleted again.
  • Deleting your account does not delete data stored on your own devices. The Account page offers to clear the current browser too.

An e-mail address is needed to have an account, because it is how you sign in and how we tell you about security events. If you do not want to give one, you can use the app without an account.

Larger project files and our encrypted weekly backups are kept in our file storage in the European Union (Frankfurt, Germany). If you are in the UK or the EU, storing your account data in the United States is a transfer outside the UK and the EU. Our staff may access account data from the UAE to run the service.
  • Safeguards. Our hosting provider processes the request logs under its own data processing terms. Before we invite anyone outside H2Oil to the account trial, our contracts with the hosting and e-mail processors will include the safeguards the law requires for a controller outside the UK and the EU and for storing account data in the United States, such as the EU standard contractual clauses and the UK Addendum or the UK International Data Transfer Agreement.

  • Analytics. If you accept analytics, Google may process Google Analytics data in the United States and other countries. Google states that it protects these transfers with safeguards such as the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, its UK extension, or standard contractual clauses.
  • Sign-in. Google and LinkedIn handle their own transfers for sign-in under their own privacy policies.

Once these safeguards are in place, you can ask us for a copy of them (with commercial details removed).

16. Security

  • All connections use HTTPS.
  • Project contents and project details are encrypted at rest with a separate key for each account. E-mail addresses are stored encrypted, and lookups use one-way keyed fingerprints.
